### D1. Cycling: the UCI's first crash census (sport science / safety, road cycling)

**Working headline:** Why do women's races crash harder? Cycling's first crash census has early answers, and more questions

**Reader promise:** You'll learn what the UCI actually measured in its new crash study, how women's and men's WorldTour crashes differed, what is changing on the road (barriers, GPS trackers), and what the data can't tell us yet.

**Seed source (OPENED)**
- *UCI Management Committee met in Montréal and took several key decisions for the 2027 season*. UCI press release, dated 23 Sep 2026 (the meeting ran 20–22 Sep).
  URL: https://www.uci.org/pressrelease/uci-management-committee-met-in-montreal-and-took-several-key-decisions-for-the-2027-season/3j98hCXlGoOJKJn5EyZiLM
- The crash-study passage, verbatim: "After five months of monitoring, the first data from the road cycling crash study, conducted since March 2026 at UCI WorldTour and UCI Women's WorldTour events, show that injuries are generally more frequent and more severe among female riders than male riders, with a return to competition that takes on average twice as long." The release also says the study is "ongoing" and is building a "reference database".
- The same release covers:
  - New finish-barrier rules. Barriers in "the final 200 metres before race finishes" must meet standards from a University of Applied Sciences and Arts Western Switzerland Valais-Wallis study. This applies to WorldTour and Women's WorldTour events from 2027.
  - GPS tracking. It has been used for all categories at Kigali 2025, is being used again at Montréal 2026, and "the protocols, the relevant regulatory framework and the implementation timeline will be developed as quickly as possible."

**Companion to the seed, for the numbers (OPENED)**
- *Female cyclists 50% more likely to be injured in crashes and suffer 2.5 times more fractures, new UCI study finds*. Cyclingnews, Laura Weislo, 24 Sep 2026. Reports UCI Medical Director Xavier Bigard's presentation at the UCI Congress.
  URL: https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/safety/uci-study-on-pro-cycling-crashes-finds-disproportionate-injury-risk-for-womens-worldtour-riders/
- Sample:
  - 21 men's races (90 days) and 14 women's events (37 days).
  - 12,951 men's rider-race days and 4,381 women's rider-race days.
- Results for women compared with men:
  - Crashes involved on average 4.7 riders in women's races and 2.7 in men's.
  - Incident rate about 1.3x higher. Incidents needing medical intervention 1.5x higher. Injuries 1.55x higher.
  - 4.3x more head-level injuries, 2.3x more concussions, "nearly 3 times" more severe skin lesions and about 2.5x more fractures.
- Crashes on descents were the most severe for both sexes. Men crashed less in the rain. No contributing factor was identified for women.
- Bigard's caveats:
  - "these are only preliminary data".
  - Bone health is "a clear hypothesis" but "we are not certain".
  - Rider height, weight and frame geometry are not in the dataset.
- Estimated injury cost: about €891,000 for women and about €2.3m for men. The article says this was "not normalised".

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Fallon, Palmer, Bigard, Heron. *Epidemiology of injury and illness across all the competitive cycling disciplines: a systematic review and meta-analysis*. BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine, 18 Jul 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12278157/
   - Road cycling: 3.68 injuries per 365 days, and the highest fracture prevalence (17.84%).
   - The authors flag scarce sex-disaggregated data and possible under-reporting in women.
   - Use this as context only: it is older, and it shows why a prospective UCI dataset is new.
2. *UCI to require safer barriers along race finishes in 2027 and introduce GPS tracking rules*. Cyclingnews, Laura Weislo, 4 Sep 2026. https://www.cyclingnews.com/pro-cycling/safety/uci-to-require-safer-barriers-along-race-finishes-in-2027-seven-years-after-fabio-jakobsens-tour-de-pologne-crash/
   - GPS trackers were used for all riders at Kigali 2025.
   - A 2025 test at the women's Tour de Romandie drew controversy: several teams refused to take part, and there were disagreements over who supplies and runs the trackers.

**Investigated, not used**
- *New UCI study offers statistical look at crashes*. Escape Collective, 24 Sep 2026 (OPENED). It repeats the same presentation with fewer figures.
- UCI press release *The UCI provides update on SafeR's activities for safety in road races* (OPENED). The page is undated and describes 2025 tests (Guangxi gear-ratio trial, 2025 yellow-card data). It is older and superseded by the 23 Sep 2026 release.
- CBC Radio, *Professional cyclists can top 100 km/h…* (BLOCKED, 403).

**Segment outline**
1. **The pile-up gap.** 4.7 riders per women's crash against 2.7 per men's crash, the headline contrast.
2. **What the UCI actually counted.** March–August 2026, WorldTour and Women's WorldTour races, rider-race days, and what an "incident" means (any crash or fall, injured or not).
3. **More often, and worse.** The 1.3x / 1.5x / 1.55x rates, then the severity figures (head 4.3x, concussion 2.3x, fractures ~2.5x) and a return to racing that takes twice as long.
4. **Why? Hypotheses, not answers.** The bone-health hypothesis (hormones, low energy availability, a sport with no ground impact). Variation in skill level. Bike geometry that the dataset doesn't capture. Descents as the constant danger.
5. **Reading ratios carefully.** 37 race days against 90. Rates against counts. Preliminary and not peer-reviewed. Earlier research had little data on women.
6. **What changes now.** Certified barriers in the final 200 m from 2027, the push for GPS trackers, and the study continuing as a reference database.

**Risks and no-go claims**
- **Preliminary status.** The data are preliminary and unpublished, and the detailed figures come from a presentation as reported by Cyclingnews. Only the qualitative summary appears in the UCI's own text.
- **Causation.** Don't write "women crash because…". No causal factor was identified for women, and the bone-health idea is explicitly uncertain. Attribute Bigard's "heterogeneity in skill level" remark to him and give its context, so it isn't framed as a judgement on women riders.
- **Barrier distance.** The 4 Sep Cyclingnews piece says the final 500 m. The UCI's 23 Sep release says the final 200 m. Use the UCI wording.
- **Cost figures.** The €891k and €2.3m estimates are "not normalised". Don't compare them directly.
- **Named riders.** Don't name riders who died. Refer to "a fatal crash at the 2024 Worlds" only if it's needed.

### D2. Rugby: smart mouthguards and the "one-size head" (sport tech / biomechanics)

**Working headline:** One head size fits all? Why smart mouthguards may misjudge hits to women and kids

**Reader promise:** You'll see how a smart mouthguard turns a tackle into a number, and why the head size it assumes can move that number by more than half.

**Seed source (OPENED: PubMed abstract and Crossref metadata; full text not read)**
- Bussey MD, McGeown JP, Dempsey S. *Sensitivity of brain injury criteria to anthropometric scaling assumptions in instrumented mouthguard data*. Journal of Biomechanics 2026 Jul;204:113378. Published online 17 May 2026, CC BY 4.0.
  - DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiomech.2026.113378. PMID 42155167.
  - Abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42155167/
- Findings (abstract):
  - The data are 15,237 video-verified head acceleration events from 572 community rugby players, aged 10–38 and weighing 34–142 kg.
  - "most commercial systems assume a 50th-percentile male head model."
  - Female players modelled with male parameters showed "up to 54% higher PRHIC and 18–27% higher HIP and kinetic energy values (p < 0.001)".
  - For players under 55 kg, the multiple-fixed-reference model "reduced predicted rotational power by more than 60%".
  - Near operational thresholds (e.g. PLA ≥ 65 g), impacts were misclassified in both sexes, and the direction of the error depended on the assumed head size.
- Conflicts of interest: Bussey has received research funding from World Rugby, and Dempsey is employed by GE HealthCare.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Ward et al. *Biomechanical Profiles of Diagnosed Concussions in Rugby Union: A Case Study Using Instrumented Mouthguards*. Sports Medicine – Open, 13 Jul 2026. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13365273/
   - Three diagnosed concussions over 34 matches (254 player-matches, 241 playing hours), with 4,414 head acceleration events recorded.
   - Peaks: linear acceleration 27.6–48.4 g, angular acceleration 3,234–4,089 rad/s².
   - The concussions happened at magnitudes below those commonly recorded without injury.
   - Limitation: only three cases.
2. University of Otago release via Medical Xpress, 29 Jun 2026. https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-06-size-smart-mouthguard-overlook-rugby.html
   - Plain-language summary, with a quote from Prof. Bussey on the >60% change for players under 55 kg.
   - Proposes at least three reference head models, chosen by sex and body size.
3. World Rugby, *World Rugby integrates smart mouthguard technology to the Head Injury Assessment…*, 9 Oct 2023. https://www.world.rugby/news/875212/…
   - Background only (older). The mouthguards feed into the Head Injury Assessment from January 2024, World Rugby committed €2m, and the supplier is Prevent Biometrics.

**Investigated, not used**
- World Rugby Passport HIA protocol page (OPENED). It mentions a mouthguard alert that "has exceeded clinical threshold" but gives no numbers, so it can't support any threshold claim.
- *Assessing Head Acceleration Events in Female Community Rugby Union Players* (Sports Medicine, 5 Sep 2024; PMC11946941) (OPENED). It is older, and it already scaled data to 5th/50th-percentile female models. That is useful nuance, but it isn't new.
- Lexology and BCLP law-firm commentaries on smart mouthguards (SNIPPET ONLY; secondary).

**Segment outline**
1. **A tackle becomes a number.** What the sensor measures at the teeth, and how that is converted to the head's centre of mass.
2. **The hidden dummy.** Most commercial systems default to a crash-test head: the Hybrid III 50th-percentile adult male.
3. **Swap the head, change the verdict.** Up to 54% (female players) and more than 60% (players under 55 kg).
4. **Why it matters at pitch-side.** Threshold alerts, and misclassification in both directions.
5. **A second 2026 clue.** Diagnosed concussions happened below "big-hit" magnitudes (Ward et al.).
6. **The proposed fix, and the open questions.** Three reference heads by sex and body mass. Nothing is known yet about adoption or cost.

**Risks and no-go claims**
- The paper models and re-scales measurements. It doesn't link its numbers to diagnosed injuries, so "higher risk score" does not mean "more injuries".
- The data come from community players, not elite ones.
- I read only the abstract. Get the full text before describing the methods in detail.
- Don't quote World Rugby's alert thresholds, which I haven't verified.
- Say "most commercial systems", as the authors do, not "all". Some research already scales by sex (see the 2024 study).
- Disclose the World Rugby funding.

## DEFINE desk: "Literacy: what a term, metric or rule means, and how not to get fooled"

### F1. Football finance: Squad Cost Ratio (rules / finance)

**Working headline:** Squad Cost Ratio: one name, two rulebooks, and why 85% isn't 70%

**Reader promise:** You'll know exactly what counts as "squad cost", why the Premier League and UEFA limits differ, and how to read a "breach" headline without being misled.

**Seed source (OPENED)**
- *New Premier League financial system explained*. Premier League, 21 Jul 2026.
  URL: https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4467022/new-premier-league-financial-system-explained
- What counts:
  - SCR "limits Premier League clubs' on-pitch spending to 85% of their football-related revenue and net profit/loss from player sales".
  - Squad costs are player and head-coach wages, agents' fees, and amortisation or impairment of transfer fees.
  - Administrative staff and assistant coaches are excluded.
  - Income from women's teams and academies counts, but their costs don't.
- When it is tested: a compliance test on 1 March, monitoring in October, and post-season Accounts Confirmation Tests.
- Thresholds and allowance:
  - The Green Threshold is 85%. The Red Threshold starts at 115%, because every club gets a 30% allowance.
  - Worked example: an SCR of 100% in one season cuts the next season's allowance to 15%.
  - A "Positive Feedback Loop" gives back 10% after a compliant season.
- Penalties:
  - Levy worked example: an overspend of £250,000 at a ratio of 89% gives a £10,000 levy.
  - Going over the Red Threshold costs a "fixed six-point deduction, which increases by one point for every £6.5m spent over the Red Threshold".
  - Levies apply only to breaches from 2027/28 onwards.
- The separate SSR tests:
  - Working capital of at least £12.5m.
  - Liquidity after an £85m stress test.
  - A Positive Equity Ratio of ≤90% in 2026/27, falling to 85% and then 80%.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. UEFA, *Finalisation of club monitoring for the 2025/26 season*, 30 Jun 2026. https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/02a6-20fe598acc34-5b167fbb1b19-1000--finalisation-of-club-monitoring-for-the-2025-26-season/
   - Nine clubs reported ratios above 70% for calendar year 2025: Strasbourg (€25m fine), Aston Villa (€22.5m), Fenerbahçe (€7m), Fiorentina (€6m), Chelsea (€3m), Newcastle (€3m), Nottingham Forest (€2.5m), AEK Athens (€0.5m) and Nice (€0.45m).
   - Parts of some fines are conditional.
   - Clubs with a "significant breach" face restrictions on registering new players to List A for 2026/27.
   - Bologna and Napoli were "fully mitigated" by football-earnings surpluses.
2. UEFA Club Licensing and Financial Sustainability Regulations, 2026 edition, section K.2 "Squad cost ratio denominator". https://documents.uefa.com/r/UEFA-Club-Licensing-and-Financial-Sustainability-Regulations-2026/K.2-Squad-cost-ratio-denominator-Online
   - The denominator is adjusted operating revenue plus net profit or loss on player disposals, impairment, and other transfer income or expenses.
   - So UEFA's measure is not simply "total revenue".
3. Premier League statement, 21 Nov 2025. https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4468470/premier-league-statement-new-financial-system
   - Clubs voted SCR and SSR in, and "insufficient support" sank Top-to-Bottom Anchoring.
4. Optional: UEFA, *New report highlights record revenues…*, 26 Feb 2026.
   - Player wage growth has "stabilised at 2–3% annually", "aided by the implementation of squad cost regulations".

**Investigated, not used**
- *The Analysis Series* on theesk.org (Feb, May and Jul 2026) (SNIPPET ONLY). A fan-blog analysis, so secondary.
- Lagom Sports Compliance, Cholismo Lab and footballfinancelab explainers (SNIPPET ONLY). Commercial or secondary explainers that duplicate the primaries.
- Yahoo, *Four Premier League clubs fined by Uefa…* (SNIPPET ONLY). Duplicates UEFA's own decision.

**Segment outline**
1. **The one-line definition.** Squad costs ÷ football revenue.
2. **What's on top of the fraction.** Wages, agents' fees and amortisation, with amortisation explained in one sentence. Then what is left out.
3. **What's underneath.** Player-sale profits count. Women's and academy income counts, but their costs don't.
4. **Two thresholds, two calendars.** The Premier League uses 85% per season. UEFA uses 70% per calendar year. That's how a club can be fine at home and fined in Europe (UEFA's June 2026 list).
5. **Traffic lights.** Green, Red, levy and points, walked through with the league's own examples.
6. **How not to get fooled.** A "breach" doesn't automatically mean points. A UEFA fine is not a Premier League breach. Levies only bite from 2027/28. SSR is a separate test.

**Risks and no-go claims**
- The Premier League page calls itself a "simplified summary". The binding text is in Handbook Appendices 2 and 3, which I haven't opened.
- The Premier League page describes UEFA's limit as "70% of its total revenue". That simplifies UEFA's actual denominator, so use the K.2 wording.
- Don't predict which clubs will breach.
- Name the clubs UEFA sanctioned only as UEFA reported them, and note the conditional parts of the fines.

### F2. Baseball: ABS challenges and "overturn rate" (analytics / rules)

**Working headline:** The robot umpire's scoreboard: why "overturn rate" is baseball's new batting average

**Reader promise:** You'll learn how MLB's ball-strike challenge works, and how to tell who is genuinely good at it without being fooled by tiny samples and last-ditch challenges.

**Seed source (OPENED)**
- Mike Petriello, *Think you know who's been good at using ABS? It's not so simple*. MLB.com, 30 Apr 2026.
  URL: https://www.mlb.com/news/how-to-know-who-is-good-at-using-abs-2026-mlb
- The key line, verbatim: "Consider overturn rate the batting average of ABS metrics".
- Figures:
  - Challenges came on roughly 1% of pitches.
  - The average overturn rate was 53%, ranging from 63% to 39% across teams.

2. **The zone the machine uses.** 53.5% and 27% of height, flat and mid-plate. Not the rulebook's 3D box, and not the umpire's zone.

- The "expected" metrics come from MLB's own models, and MLB.com is league-owned media. Frame them as that.
- Don't claim the tracking system is error-free. I haven't opened any source on its accuracy.
- Avoid ranking named players on small samples. If players are named at all, keep it to public stats.

### F3. Heat: WBGT explained (sport science / rules, applies across sports)

**Working headline:** WBGT, explained: the heat number that decides when sport should stop

**Reader promise:** You'll learn what wet-bulb globe temperature measures, why 26, 28 and 32 °C mean different things to different organisations, and why analyses of the same World Cup produced different counts.

**Seed source (OPENED)**
- *World Cup matches played in potentially dangerous heat as players' union makes climate warning*. The Guardian, 30 Jun 2026.
  URL: https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/30/world-cup-matches-extreme-heat
  (Fetched with curl; the Guardian domain is blocked to the search tool.)
- Findings:
  - Of the 72 group-stage games, "an estimated nine matches were played in conditions of severe heat" (WBGT at or over 28 °C). Another 13 were in cities beyond that level but in air-conditioned stadiums.
  - The hottest was Uruguay v Cape Verde in Miami on 21 June, where WBGT "may have reached or exceeded 33C".
- Method: WBGT was approximated from air temperature and humidity (Open-Meteo) using an Australian Bureau of Meteorology formula.
- Thresholds and context:
  - Above a WBGT of 32 °C, FIFA says cooling breaks should be introduced and delays considered.
  - Only Dallas, Houston and Atlanta were fully cooled.
  - OSHA flags a severe risk for manual labour above 25 °C.
  - FIFA didn't answer when asked which WBGT would trigger a definite delay.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. World Weather Attribution, *Climate change big player at FIFA World Cup 2026*, 14 May 2026. https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/climate-change-big-player-at-fifa-world-cup-2026/
   - Projections: 26 games expected at ≥26 °C WBGT, 9 of them in stadiums without cooling (21 and 6 in 1994). Five games expected at ≥28 °C (3 in 1994).
   - Method: ERA5-Land temperature and humidity only. The results "apply best to a sheltered and shaded area, without the heating effect of direct sunlight or the cooling effect of wind".
   - Not peer-reviewed.
2. FIFPRO heat guidelines (PDF, created Jul 2023). https://media.fifpro.org/media/ltio1czh/heat-guidelines_en-1.pdf
   - WBGT above 26 °C (or ambient above 30 °C) should mean cooling breaks.
   - WBGT above 28 °C (or ambient above 36 °C) should mean a delay or postponement.
   - WBGT should be measured on site.
3. Mullan et al., *Extreme heat risk and the potential implications for the scheduling of football matches at the 2026 FIFA World Cup*. International Journal of Biometeorology, 25 Jan 2025. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947059/
   - Method: the Liljegren model on ERA5 data, which includes solar radiation and wind.
   - 14 of 16 host locations exceed the thresholds.
   - In Miami, afternoon hours were ≥28 °C about 80% of the time, against 7% for 6–9 pm kick-offs.
4. FIFA, *Players to benefit from hydration breaks at FIFA World Cup 2026*, 7 Dec 2025. https://inside.fifa.com/organisation/news/hydration-breaks-world-cup-2026-player-welfare
