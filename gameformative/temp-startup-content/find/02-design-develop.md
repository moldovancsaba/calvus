## DESIGN — "How to build: team, tactic, training system, experience"

### DESIGN 1 — Training system (swimming)

**Working headline:** Sleep in, swim faster? What happened when a 7 a.m. session moved to 9 a.m.
**Reader promise:** You'll see exactly what one extra hour of sleep did (and didn't do) for young national-level swimmers, and how to think about when to put early sessions.

**Seed: OPENED**
- Brandts MME, Forster S, Hecksteden A, Meyer T. "Delaying Early Morning Training Time in Swimmers: Effects on Sleep, Recovery and Performance — A Randomized Controlled Trial." *Sports Medicine – Open*, published 28 Jul 2026. DOI 10.1186/s40798-026-01086-x. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415721/
- What I read: randomized controlled crossover trial with 27 national-level swimmers (13 female, 14 male; mean age 15.6 ± 1.0 y; Germany). There were two 8-day training camps, in January and October 2023, and the morning session started at 07:00 in one camp and 09:00 in the other. Actigraphy sleep was 5.9 ± 0.6 h (early) vs 6.9 ± 0.8 h (late), a difference of 1.0 ± 0.5 h (p < 0.001). Perceived sleep quality did not differ (p = 0.50). There were no condition effects on recovery/stress items (p > 0.15) and no effects on 100 m (p > 0.29) or 800 m (p > 0.20) performance. Authors' conclusion: the delay "increased sleep duration by one hour on average, but this did not significantly affect recovery or performance". Limitations they state: the short study period may have been too brief for effects to show, and naps were excluded from the mediation analysis.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Zheng et al. "The effect of daytime napping on the athletic performance of team ball sports athletes: systematic review and meta-analysis." *Frontiers in Physiology*, 22 Jul 2026. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2026.1878224. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13439405/ — 12 RCTs, 179 athletes (soccer, rugby, handball, basketball, volleyball). Napping had a large effect on endurance (SMD 0.96, 95% CI 0.62–1.29) and a moderate effect on agility (SMD 0.55, 0.01–1.09). Power/strength was inconclusive (SMD 0.50, −0.09–1.09), and cognitive and technical outcomes were uncertain. Naps mostly started around 14:00. Trials were small (typically 10–25 athletes) and mostly young men, and the authors call the results "promising initial estimates".
2. Jin Y et al. "Physical recovery modalities in competitive and elite endurance athletes: a systematic review and network meta-analysis finding limited evidence of benefit over passive recovery." *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, 18 Aug 2026. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1876924. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13531764/ — 16 RCTs, 273 participants. "No modality has been shown to outperform passive recovery on any outcome examined". For example, compression on performance was SMD 0.04 (−0.37 to 0.45), the only moderate-certainty comparison. 31 of 47 comparisons were very low certainty.

**Investigated, not used**
- "Written vs. verbal sleep hygiene strategies administration in track and field athletes: a randomized controlled trial" (*J Sports Med Phys Fitness*, Sept 2026 issue). **SNIPPET ONLY**: I did not open it because the Minerva full text sits behind a paywall. It is also about sleep education, not training time.
- Esteve-Lanao et al., polarized vs pyramidal TID in recreational marathoners (SAGE, 2026). https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17479541261468168 — **BLOCKED (403)**. It is also off-topic (intensity distribution, not scheduling).
- IOC consensus statement on mental health in elite athletes (BJSM, published 12 Aug 2026 per search results; IOC news page timed out). I did not read it. It belongs to a wellbeing/health desk, not scheduling.

**Segment outline (target ~2,000–2,800 chars)**
1. **The 6 a.m. alarm problem**: why early sessions are a swimming tradition, and the trade-off with teenage sleep.
2. **The experiment**: same swimmers, two camps, 07:00 vs 09:00 starts, with sleep measured by wrist actigraphy.
3. **What changed: an hour of sleep**: 5.9 h → 6.9 h, while perceived sleep quality stayed the same.
4. **What didn't change: times and recovery scores**: 100 m and 800 m showed no significant effect. Explain "no significant effect ≠ proven no effect" over 8 days.
5. **The bigger recovery picture**: napping evidence in team sports, and the finding that gadgets haven't beaten plain rest in endurance athletes.
6. **How to build it into a week**: design questions for coaches (e.g. pool availability, school schedules, where naps fit), not prescriptions.

**Risks / must not overclaim**
- Don't say "sleep doesn't matter" or "later training is useless". The study lasted 8 days, had n = 27, and involved adolescents at camp. It was not powered to rule out small effects.
- Don't generalise to adults or other sports. The napping review mostly covers young men and small trials.
- The swimmers are minors, but none are named. Keep it that way.
- The data were collected in 2023 and published in 2026. Say "published this summer", not "this summer's camp".

### DESIGN 2 — Experience + tactic (baseball)

**Working headline:** Two taps on the helmet: how baseball's robot-umpire challenge changed strategy in year one
**Reader promise:** How MLB designed a challenge system that keeps human umpires, and what a full season of data says about who wins challenges and why.

**Seed: OPENED**
- ESPN (Jacob Nitzberg, with other contributors). "MLB ABS challenge rankings: Best and worst of 2026." Published 23 Sep 2026; data through games of 22 Sep 2026. https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/50013483/mlb-abs-challenges-best-worst-2026
- What I read: the MLB average challenge success rate was 54%. Batter challenges succeeded 49% of the time and pitcher/catcher challenges 58.1%. Best overall team: Reds 61.5%. Worst: Pirates 48.1%. Best on defence: Phillies at 67.6%. Worst batter challenges: Rockies at 41.4%. Catcher William Contreras had 94 successful challenges from 172 attempts, and J.T. Realmuto was 62 of 78 (79.5%). The article does not give total challenges, challenges per game or time per challenge.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. MLB press release, "MLB announces ABS Challenge System coming to the Major Leagues beginning in the 2026 season", 23 Sep 2025. https://www.mlb.com/press-release/press-release-mlb-announces-abs-challenge-system-coming-to-the-major-leagues-beginning-in-the-2026-season — The rules:
   - Each team gets two challenges per game, and "All successful challenges are retained".
   - "Only the pitcher, catcher or batter may challenge". A challenge is called by tapping the hat or helmet and saying so.
   - The process takes about 15 seconds.
   - A team gets one extra challenge in each extra inning if it has none left.
   - The zone is 17 in wide, with the top at 53.5% and the bottom at 27% of the batter's height.
   - 2025 spring training: just over 4 challenges per game, and a success rate near 50%.
2. ESPN (Bradford Doolittle), "MLB 2026: What we've learned so far about ABS", 19 May 2026. https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/48807610/mlb-2026-abs-automated-balls-strikes-system-early-numbers-lessons-analytics — Framing value among the top-30 framers fell about 20% (0.704 → 0.565 runs per 100 innings caught). The walk rate was 9.4%, described as the highest in more than 25 years. These are May figures only.
3. MLB.com (Mike Petriello), "Players, teams that have been successful at ABS challenges", 30 Apr 2026. https://www.mlb.com/news/how-to-know-who-is-good-at-using-abs-2026-mlb — Early-season overturn rate was 53% and challenges came on about 1% of pitches. Key point: overturn rate "is missing a ton of context", which is why he also uses run value.

**Investigated, not used**
- Baseball-Reference, "2026 MLB ABS Challenge Analysis". https://www.baseball-reference.com/friv/abs-challenges.shtml — **BLOCKED (403)**. The figure "8,958 challenges / 4,810 overturned through Sept 3" is **SNIPPET ONLY**. Do not use it.
- Baseball Savant ABS dashboard (MLB's own data). https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/abs?gameType=regular&year=2026&level=mlb — The dashboard is rendered with JavaScript, so no data was readable. Not used. It would be the ideal primary source if an editor can open it in a browser.
- Yahoo/FanSided, "MLB's ABS challenge data just proved what hitters have known for years" (batting-order slot claims). **SNIPPET ONLY**, not opened. It is a secondary take.

**Segment outline (target ~2,200–3,000 chars)**
1. **The design brief**: fix obvious misses without replacing the umpire, and keep the game moving.
2. **The rules in 30 seconds**: 2 challenges, kept if you win, only the three players involved can call one, about 15 seconds, and the height-based zone.
3. **Year-one scoreboard**: 54% overall, and why fielders (58.1%) beat batters (49%). Vantage point and which calls each side challenges.
4. **The catcher's new job**: framing value shrinks (May data) while challenge judgement becomes a skill.
5. **Challenge as tactic**: "save it for leverage". Why success rate alone misleads (run-value point).
6. **What other sports can steal from it**: a limited-challenge design compared with full automation (keep this short, as a design lens).

**Risks / must not overclaim**
- The regular season isn't finished. Every number is "through 22 Sep 2026", so re-check before publishing.
- Walk-rate and framing figures are from May. Don't present them as full-season numbers or claim ABS caused the walk increase.
- Success rate is not the same as value (Petriello), so don't rank teams as "best at ABS" on rate alone.
- ESPN is secondary reporting of Statcast data. Label it that way, and if possible verify in Baseball Savant in a browser.

### DESIGN 3 — Training system (football, injury prevention)

**Working headline:** Nordic curls vs FIFA 11+: the best hamstring programme is the one players actually do
**Reader promise:** What two 2026 meta-analyses say about which hamstring-prevention programmes work, why they seem to disagree, and the one factor they both point to.

**Seed: OPENED**
- Hong B, Hong X, Lin H, Yang Z, Feng J. "Comparative Effectiveness of Exercise Interventions for Hamstring Injury Prevention in Football Players: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis." *Sports Medicine – Open*, 15 Sep 2026. DOI 10.1186/s40798-026-01087-w. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13578199/
- What I read: 11 RCTs with 9,282 players, published 2008–2023.
  - Only FIFA 11+ beat usual training: IRR 0.55 (0.31–0.98).
  - Nordic hamstring exercise alone: IRR 0.78 (0.34–1.78), not significant. FIFA 11+ Kids: 0.43 (0.08–2.32), not significant. FIFA 11: 0.99. Bounding: 1.18.
  - P-scores: FIFA 11+ 0.84, 11+ Kids 0.78, NHE 0.56.
  - GRADE certainty was low to very low. Heterogeneity was I² = 72%. No trial was at overall low risk of bias. Only 2 female-only trials were included.
  - The network is star-shaped, with no head-to-head trials. The authors say the ranking is "not … a basis for firm recommendations".

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Gu J, Li Y, Wang Y, Zhang Y. "Intervention components, training dose, and adherence in exercise-based prevention of hamstring strain injury in football: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Annals of Medicine*, 7 Apr 2026. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13059038/
   - 15 RCTs with 7,465 players. All programmes pooled: RR 0.51 (0.36–0.71), about 38 fewer hamstring strains per 1,000 players. NHE-based programmes: RR 0.46 (0.26–0.84).
   - High adherence (≥75%): RR 0.36 (0.28–0.48). Low adherence: RR 0.92 (0.68–1.23), with p for interaction < 0.00001. Each +10% adherence gives an RR multiplier of 0.83.
   - Frequency and weekly volume showed no significant dose effect.
   - Certainty was low, the prediction interval crosses 1 (0.18–1.40), and 11 of 15 trials were men only.

**Investigated, not used**
- Alkishnawi et al. "Preventive efficiency of neuromuscular training in reducing lower limb injuries among youth soccer athletes" (*Front Sports Act Living*, 8 Jul 2026). DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1853354. **OPENED (abstract)**. It covers all lower-limb injuries in players aged 8–18, with 5 RCTs, and the pooled result was not significant (IRR 0.54, 0.20–1.45). It is broader, weaker and duplicative for a hamstring-specific piece.
- "Why methods matter in a meta-analysis: a reappraisal showed inconclusive injury preventive effect of Nordic hamstring exercise" (PubMed 34520846). **SNIPPET ONLY**, from 2021, older. Useful background for an editor, but not read.
- Tedeschi et al. 2025, SAGE, on the NHE in football. Not opened. It is older and a narrative piece.

**Segment outline (target ~2,000–3,000 chars)**
1. **Football's most annoying injury**: why hamstring strains are the target (keep it general, no invented incidence numbers).
2. **Two reviews, two headlines**: one finds NHE programmes cut risk by about half, the other finds NHE-alone not significant against usual training.
3. **Why they differ**: different trial sets (15 vs 11), pooled vs network comparison, no head-to-head trials, low certainty.
4. **Where they agree**: whole-programme warm-ups (FIFA 11+) look consistently useful, and adherence is the big modifier (≥75% vs <75%).
5. **Build it so it gets done**: feasibility over novelty, and short blocks. Duration finding: 1–6 months RR 0.43 vs 7–10 months RR 0.77, which is study-level, not causal.
6. **What we still don't know**: the women's football gap, lack of head-to-head trials, and reporting of dose.

**Risks / must not overclaim**
- Don't say "Nordic curls don't work". Say "the NHE-alone estimate is imprecise in this network".
- Both reviews are low or very low certainty, so frame everything as associations.
- Subgroup and adherence findings are study-level (ecological), not proof that any one player's adherence causes protection.
- Nearly all trials are in men. Say so explicitly and don't extrapolate to women.

## DEVELOP — "Pathways: athletes and coaches getting better over time"

### DEVELOP 1 — Talent systems (multi-sport, 42 countries)

**Working headline:** The revolving door: about a third of every talent squad changes each year
**Reader promise:** What a 44,287-athlete meta-analysis reveals about how often talent programmes pick, drop and replace young athletes, and what that means for families, coaches and federations.

**Seed: OPENED**
- Güllich A, Barth M. "Annual Athlete Turnover of 36% in Olympic Talent Promotion Programs: A Meta-analysis." *Sports Medicine – Open*, e-published 30 Jun 2026. DOI 10.1186/s40798-026-01050-9.
  - Read via PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13319627/, and via the Europe PMC abstract record.
  - The Springer landing page https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-026-01050-9 was **BLOCKED** by a JS challenge.
- What I read:
  - 37 samples published 2010–25, covering 44,287 athletes from all Olympic sports and 42 countries.
  - Mean annual turnover was 36.3% (95% CI 32.7–39.8), defined as (entries + exits) / 2 / current athletes.
  - Federation junior squads: 39.9% (35.2–44.8, k = 17). Youth academies: 32.8% (28.1–37.6, k = 20). p = 0.039. No difference by age or sex.
  - Among studies reporting both, entries averaged 36.6% and exits 32.1% per year.
  - "More than half of the TPP population is exchanged every two years."
  - Higher-performing seniors entered programmes later than lower-performing seniors.
  - Implications they list: postpone early selection, strengthen community clubs, evaluate programmes on senior outcomes, and monitor physical and psychosocial costs.
  - Limitations: 83% of athletes were male, Western Europe is over-represented, soccer dominates the federation data, and there are no Paralympic studies.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Thieschäfer L, Schorer J, Granacher U, Büsch D. "The Role of Training Age and Sport Specialization in Achieved Selection Tier in Adolescent Handball Talents." *Sports Medicine – Open*, 11 Aug 2026. DOI 10.1186/s40798-026-01079-w. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13461995/
   - 1,811 German players (879 female U15, 932 male U16).
   - Multisport engagement was not associated with selection tier (p = 0.567 female, p = 0.805 male).
   - Youth national team players had more years in handball than non-selected players: +0.86 y for females (g = 0.59) and +0.73 y for males (g = 0.40).
   - The authors call multisport "a viable alternative developmental pathway". The study is cross-sectional, and training age is measured in years, not hours.
2. Hagiwara M et al. "Structural and developmental characteristics of Japanese elite athletes: a longitudinal case study of a talent identification and development programme in Fukuoka prefecture." *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, 9 Feb 2026. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1748585. **OPENED (abstract via Europe PMC)**. Full text is open access at PMC12926450; read it before citing detail.
   - 383 graduates, of whom 31 became senior elite. 90% of those had experienced a sport transition.
   - Early RAE and fitness advantages "diminished by the senior elite level".

**Investigated, not used**
- Kelly AL et al. "Principles of talent identification in sport: The TIDES Society position statement." *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 20 Aug 2026. DOI 10.1080/02640414.2026.2705709. **OPENED (abstract)**, full text paywalled. It lists 13 principles, including "entry and exit points". It is relevant, but it offers principles, not data. Cite it only if an editor gets the full text.
- Lundberg TR et al. "Evidence-Based Insights for the Development and Talent Promotion of Young Athletes in a National Sports Context." *Sports Medicine*, 13 Sep 2026. DOI 10.1007/s40279-026-02535-z. **OPENED (abstract)**, paywalled. It is a perspective article focused on Sweden, not new data.
- Chen R et al. "Beyond the youth success trap: a Four-Gate Coaching Audit of Japan's 2017 U20 football cohort." *Front Sports Act Living*, 21 Aug 2026. **OPENED (abstract)**. It is a proof-of-concept with n = 21 named national-team players, so the piece would become about individuals. Not used.

**Segment outline (target ~2,200–3,000 chars)**
1. **The promise of a talent programme**: pick early, nurture continuously.
2. **What 44,287 athletes show**: 36% turnover a year, and more than half the squad swapped within two years.
3. **Squads vs academies**: federation squads churn more (40% vs 33%). Age and sex made no difference.
4. **Side doors are real**: later entrants, athletes who switched sports (Fukuoka), and handball multisport players who were not disadvantaged.
5. **What this means for a 13-year-old who's been cut (or picked)**: selection is provisional in both directions.
6. **Designing kinder, smarter systems**: delay selection, invest in clubs, judge programmes by senior outcomes, and count the costs to those who are dropped.

**Risks / must not overclaim**
- Turnover is not the same as failure. The paper infers inefficiency and inaccurate identification, but it does not measure individual outcomes, so frame that as the authors' interpretation.
- The samples are mostly male, Western European and team-sport (63%). Say so.
- Don't imply that every programme turns over 36%. It is a mean, and it varies by type.
- Seed e-pub date is 30 Jun 2026, one day before the preferred July–Sept window. It is within the allowed range. If a strict July–Sept seed is required, swap in DEVELOP 2 as the lead.

### DEVELOP 2 — Relative age across a pathway (basketball, with rugby and football context)

**Working headline:** The birthday advantage that fades: following relative age from French youth basketball to the senior national team
**Reader promise:** Why January-born kids dominate youth basketball selections, and why that edge has vanished by the time players pull on a senior national-team shirt.

**Seed: OPENED**
- Irid Y, Difernand A, Toussaint J-F, Sedeaud A. "Relative age effect and development pathways in French basketball: from youth training structures to senior national teams." *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, published 10 Aug 2026 (received 28 May, accepted 27 Jul 2026). DOI 10.3389/fspor.2026.1893863. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1893863/full (also PMC13500515)
- What I read:
  - Regional training centres, Q1 vs Q4 births: females 38.31% vs 14.84%, males 43.90% vs 10.46%.
  - National teams, Q1 vs Q4:
    - Male: U16 45.53% vs 8.95%; U18 35.95% vs 12.42%; U20 36.27% vs 12.95%; senior 21.05% vs 22.11% (p = 0.58, not significant).
    - Female: U16 36.92% vs 16.92%; senior 29.82% vs 21.05% (p = 0.48, not significant).
  - Pathway origins of senior internationals:
    - Women (n = 57): 64.91% came from regional centres, and 21.05% had not been through regional or national centres.
    - Men (n = 95): 35.79% came from regional centres, and 49.47% had not been through regional or national centres.
    - The authors: women's pathway is "more linear".
  - Limitations: descriptive design, no data on maturation or training, French system only.
  - Samples: 2,399 female and 2,572 male regional centre players, 157 female and 216 male national-centre players, 1,981 female and 3,103 male club academy players, and 896 female and 803 male national-team players.

**Supporting sources**
1. **OPENED**: Brustio PR, Cobley S, … Till K, Kelly AL. "Relative age effects in Rugby union and Rugby league: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Front Sports Act Living*, 5 Jun 2026. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1809947/full
   - 26 studies, covering 318,663 male and 41,379 female players.
   - Male Q1 vs Q4: OR 1.99, or 1.32 after publication-bias adjustment. Youth OR 2.42, senior OR 1.41.
   - Female: OR 1.15 overall and 1.02 at senior level (not significant).
   - Male quartile split: 34.8 / 26.1 / 22.1 / 16.9%. Heterogeneity I² = 95.6%.
2. **OPENED**: ECNL (Elite Club National League), "Center for Athlete Health and Performance examines relative age effect in youth soccer", 3 Aug 2026. Authors: Dr Matthew Andrew and Dr Drew Watson. https://theecnl.com/news/2026/8/3/general-center-for-athlete-health-and-performance-examines-relative-age-effect-in-youth-soccer.aspx
   - It gives no figures, but six recommendations, e.g. don't equate early maturity with talent, and avoid early position assignment on physical traits.
   - Watson: "Switching from birth year to school year or vice versa will not eliminate RAEs."
3. **OPENED (abstract only)**: Torrent J, Rossier L. "Temporal trends in the relative age effect in five major European men's football leagues, 2000–2025." *Science and Medicine in Football*, 2 Sep 2026. DOI 10.1080/24733938.2026.2728065. 14,173 players: Q1 30.4% vs Q4 20.1% (Cramér's V = 0.085, a small effect). The effect rose, then stabilised. Use it only as a one-line contrast ("in men's football the skew persists into the pros") and label it as from the abstract.

**Investigated, not used**
- Díaz-Almazán I et al. "Influence of the relative age effect and maturity status on competitive playing time in an elite football academy." *Front Sports Act Living*, 1 Jun 2026. **OPENED (abstract)**. It is a single academy with 172 cases, and the main RAE effects were not significant (p = .054/.057). Weaker.
- Moganan H et al. "The relative age effect among Malaysian university athletes …" *Front Sports Act Living*, 22 Jul 2026. **OPENED (abstract)**. Off-population (university athletes), and the AI movement-screening angle is exploratory.
- Han C et al. "The Effect of Bio-Banding On Talent Identification in Youth Soccer: A Systematic Review." *J Sports Sci Med*, June 2026. Seen only as search and index metadata, not opened. It answers a different question (maturity grouping). Save it for a bio-banding piece.

**Segment outline (target ~2,200–3,000 chars)**
1. **The calendar quirk**: what relative age is, in one paragraph.
2. **At 15, the skew is huge**: male U16 national team 45.5% Q1 vs 9.0% Q4.
3. **By the senior team, it's gone**: 21.1% vs 22.1% for men, and not significant for women either.
4. **Where did the seniors come from?** About half the men never went through regional or national centres, while the women's route is more linear.
5. **Not every sport forgets**: rugby men keep a smaller skew at senior level (OR 1.41), and top European men's football still shows a small skew.
6. **What clubs can actually do**: the ECNL recommendations, and why changing the cut-off date alone doesn't fix it.

**Risks / must not overclaim**
- The study is descriptive and doesn't track individuals, so don't say "late-born players catch up" as a causal story. Say "the skew disappears at senior level".
- Don't claim the RAE "reverses" in basketball. The senior difference is not significant.
- The football-leagues study was read only as an abstract, and its effect size is small. Keep it to one sentence.
- The rugby meta-analysis has very high heterogeneity and likely publication bias. Use the adjusted OR (1.32) alongside the unadjusted one.
- The ECNL item gives recommendations without data. Present it as a governing body's guidance.

### DEVELOP 3 — Coaches getting better (grassroots football, 4 countries)

**Working headline:** Can six hours of training change a Saturday-morning coach? Yes, for about a month
**Reader promise:** What a four-country field study of grassroots football coaches found about coach education, and why the good effects fade without follow-up.

**Seed: OPENED**
- Tzioumakis Y et al. (13 authors; Greece, France, UK, Spain, Australia). "Effect of a theory-based coach education program on the objectively assessed motivational climate in grassroots football across four European countries: results from the Promoting Adolescent Physical Activity (PAPA) project." *Frontiers in Psychology*, published 5 Aug 2026 (Europe PMC date). DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1563931. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485734/
- What I read:
  - Design: longitudinal quasi-experimental study (not randomized), in England, France, Spain and Greece.
  - Sample: 68 coaches (66 male, 2 female; 36 intervention, 32 control; 59 retained). Players were aged 10–15.
  - Intervention: Empowering Coaching™, about 6 hours in total, delivered as two 3-hour workshops or one 6-hour session.
  - Measurement: the climate was coded from video by blinded raters using the MMCOS tool.
  - Disempowering climate declined across the season (b = −0.26, p < 0.001). Ego-involving climate fell in a sustained way (b = −0.21, p < 0.01).
  - Empowering climate and autonomy support rose at the 1-month follow-up and then declined by season end.
  - Task-involving climate and structure showed no effect.
  - Authors: "ongoing support may be necessary to maintain empowering coaching practices across a season". Limitations: delivery fidelity was not assessed, and only 2 female coaches took part.

**Supporting sources (OPENED)**
1. Tan LQW, Barkell J, O'Connor D. "Youth participation coach learning: A scoping review." *PLOS ONE*, 18 Aug 2026. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485061/
   - Only 11 studies were found. 10 of 11 were formal programmes, and 7 were football.
   - All came from Western countries. Only 3 measured athlete outcomes, and most programmes lacked sustained post-programme support.
   - Quoted finding from an included study: coaches "were not able to significantly modify their coaching behaviours in game play".
2. Jørgensen TM, Abrahamsen FE, Gjesdal S. "Coach- and Peer-Created Motivational Climate Profiles in Youth Sport: Associations With Enjoyment and Intentions to Continue." *European Journal of Sport Science* 26(9). The article page says 12 Aug 2026, while Europe PMC lists 1 Sep 2026. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13463318/
   - N = 272 Norwegian players aged 12–14 (handball and soccer, 48.5% girls). The design is cross-sectional.
   - The high-mastery/low-performance profile had the highest enjoyment: 4.87 vs 3.88 for low mastery (d = 2.12).
   - It also had higher intention to continue (d = 0.76–0.92).
   - This gives the "why it matters" link to staying in sport.
