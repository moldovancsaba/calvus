business.direct — executive summary

One page for a classified media owner deciding to use the product, and for the owner deciding to build it. Every figure is sourced in the research, computed by the prototype on the first customer's real listings, or marked as an assumption; the value with the numbers is economics.md. Written 2026-09-20 (D41).

The situation

A classified media owner — a listing platform, a directory, a marketplace of local businesses — has gathered thousands of listings and built the site visitors use. Now one person is the whole sales and marketing department for both sides: the businesses on the listings have not been contacted, do not answer the enquiries the site sends them and have not bought a placement; the visitors save and hear nothing; the advertisers who do pay leave for reasons visible weeks earlier.

The complication

Doing it alone loses the money where it is made. Listings nobody contacts earn nothing; outbound works only if the follow-ups go out — 42 % of replies come from them — in order and within the law; a lead contacted in the first five minutes is 21× more likely to qualify (InsideSales / MIT, 2007); content that is not published does not exist in a market where Gen Z searches Instagram and TikTok ahead of Google for local businesses and AI answers cite the reviewed, structured source; replacing a churned advertiser costs a full acquisition where keeping one costs a touch. The tools on sale do one job each and leave the owner as the team.

The question

How does a classified media owner run marketing, B2B sales and retention every day for thousands of listings — without hiring, without losing control of what goes out under the site's name, and without breaking the law or the visitors' trust?

The answer — business.direct

A product that runs the three jobs from the owner's own data: marketing — content generated from the listings and delivered to every medium (posts, a digest, alerts, search pages, clips from real footage); B2B sales — every listed business scored and worked through a lawful pipeline until it manages its page and buys a placement; retention — every paying advertiser watched for the signals before a cancellation and kept with a touch. Departments the owner approves, plain-text knowledge files, a human gate on everything — it drafts, it cannot send, it stops and asks — and the owner's economics as a screen: what a listing sells for, what an advertiser costs to win and is worth, what a visitor costs to bring in, what churn costs and retention keeps, the twelve-month plan, and the next dollar ranked every week. A managing advertiser gets its own team inside the product — which is why it claims, stays and upgrades. One policy record per site and a gate in the code; a child is an age, never a name.

How the owner uses it

Twenty minutes a day on Home — what needs a person, ranked, one button each, one press for the safe ones; the inbox during the day — replies, enquiries and comments with drafted answers; the recap on Monday; the Economics screen whenever an input changes. Onboarding is a week: the site's API and channels, the policy record, the knowledge files, the templates.

The benefits

Sold inventory, visitors who come back, advertisers who stay, the next dollar ranked every week, the owner's time back, lower exposure — each measured on the screen, on the owner's numbers, replacing the benchmarks as the first cohort arrives. The product tells the truth when a lever does not pay: on the first customer's sample prices its own model shows outbound alone not covering a $49 product, and the three inputs that turn it.

Proof

ClassScout (Your Field NYC) is the example and the first buyer: the prototype runs on 253 of its real listings; its privacy policy is bound into the product; its policy record and its economics are on the screen.

The decision

Build business.direct and run it for the first customer; the first measured cohort replaces the assumptions at the end of sprint 2.
