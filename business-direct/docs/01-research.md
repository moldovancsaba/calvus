# business.direct — research

> **Status — evidence.** Written while the project was framed around the first customer's site; the vocabulary of that time (client, platform, provider, family) is kept, the product's terms are in `product-definition.md` §5. Every figure the stakeholder documents use from this round was re-verified on 2026-09-20 in `evidence.md`, which is the authority where the two differ.


*What the evidence says about the four automations and the one-person machine, with the
source behind every figure. Searched 2026-09-19. Primary sources (studies, platform
disclosures, regulators) are marked **P**; industry aggregators are marked **A** and carry
less weight — several repeat each other and cite older data. Nothing here was measured by
us except the two reference platforms (`first-customer-classscout.md`).*

## 1. The listed business that does nothing loses to the one that does

- A one-star increase in a Yelp rating raises an independent restaurant's revenue by
  **5–9 %**; chains see almost no effect — reputation matters most where nobody knows the
  brand. **P** [Luca, HBS working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1928601), [Harvard Magazine summary](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2011/10/hbs-study-finds-positive-yelp-reviews-lead-to-increased-business)
- Hotels that start **responding to reviews** receive **12 % more reviews** and their
  rating rises **0.12 stars** — because unhappy guests post fewer baseless complaints
  when they see the business answers. **P** [HBR / INFORMS study](https://hbr.org/2018/02/study-replying-to-customer-reviews-results-in-better-ratings), [INFORMS release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Want-to-improve-your-business-s-online-ratings-Make-sure-to-respond-to-reviews)
- Businesses with photos on their Google profile get **42 % more direction requests and
  35 % more website clicks** (Google's own figure, widely quoted); profiles with 100+
  photos get several times more calls and clicks (BrightLocal, 15 191 listings). **A**
  [BrightLocal / Google via re:spondelligent](https://respondelligent.com/en/google-my-business-photos/), [thestacc](https://thestacc.com/blog/gbp-photos-guide/)
- Claimed and complete profiles: claimed Google listings are quoted at **+37 % local
  visibility**; complete profiles at **2.7× more likely to be seen as reputable, 70 %
  more visits**; about **41 % of small local businesses** still run incomplete profiles.
  **A** [newmedia](https://newmedia.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics), [sqmagazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/google-my-business-statistics/), [LocaliQ review stats](https://localiq.com/blog/online-review-stats/)
- **88 % of consumers who search locally on a phone visit or call within 24 hours**
  (Think with Google, quoted); "near me" searches have grown more than 900 % since 2018.
  **A** [BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/) (1 026 US adults), [via K2Z](https://k2zdigital.com/Best-Practices-for-Google-Business-Profile-Photos)

**Reading for business.direct.** The activation automation is not a courtesy: an
unclaimed, photo-less, unanswered card is measurably worth less to the business than a
claimed one, and the platform can show the business exactly that.

## 2. Speed: the machine answers first

- **MIT / InsideSales lead-response study** (2007; 6 companies, 15 000+ leads, 100 000+
  call attempts): the odds of *qualifying* a lead are **21× higher** when contacted within
  5 minutes than after 30; the odds of *contacting* at all drop **100×** between 5 and 30
  minutes. **P** [the study PDF](https://25649.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)
- **HBR** (2.24 million leads): firms that respond within an hour are **~7× more likely
  to qualify** the lead than those waiting an hour more; waiting 24 hours makes it **60×
  less likely**. **P** via [Kixie](https://www.kixie.com/sales-blog/speed-to-lead-response-time-statistics-that-drive-conversions/), [LeadAngel](https://www.leadangel.com/blog/operations/lead-response-time/)
- **62 % of calls to small businesses go unanswered** (411 Locals, 2016: 85 businesses,
  58 industries, 30 days: 37.8 % answered, 37.8 % voicemail, 24.3 % no response); **85 %
  of missed callers do not call back**; automated missed-call text-back is quoted to
  recover most of them. **A** [SkipCalls on the 411 Locals study](https://skipcalls.com/blog/percentage-business-calls-unanswered-statistics-2026), [getaira](https://www.getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics)
- **78 % of customers buy from the business that responds first**; SMS open rates ~98 %.
  **A** [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/missed-call-statistics)

**Reading.** "Someone tags you — first, not a queue" (video 1) has numbers behind it.
Reviews, messages, missed calls and booking requests are the department the business
sees value from in the first week.

## 3. Reminders and rhythm: the cheapest revenue

- A review of **29 studies**: automated reminders improve non-attendance by **29 %**;
  studios report no-shows falling from 20–30 % to **8–12 %** with SMS sequences; a
  reminder three days *and* one day before beats a single one. **P/A** [Glofox on the PubMed review](https://www.glofox.com/blog/gym-no-show-rate/), [DailyStory](https://www.dailystory.com/blog/sms-marketing-for-gyms-how-to-drive-membership-and-engagement-with-text-campaigns/)
- Trial-to-member conversion is quoted to improve **15–20 points** when the first reply
  arrives within 5 minutes. **A** [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/best-lead-management-software-gyms-fitness-studios-2026)
- The European fitness market: **75.5 million members** at end-2025 (+4.1 million),
  revenue **€39.1 bn (+9.1 %)** — the category the sport platform lists most (148 gyms of
  431) is growing. **A** [Athletech on the EuropeActive report](https://athletechnews.com/european-fitness-market-2025-more-members-more-revenue-room-to-grow-report-finds/)

**Reading.** DiscountDirect's research already established the replenishment-reminder
lever for products; for listed businesses the equivalent is the session reminder and the
"this week near you" rhythm.

## 4. Digests and alerts to consumers

- Email benchmarks 2025: open rates **35–45 %** by industry (inflated by Apple Mail
  Privacy Protection — **click rate is the honest metric**, 1.9–3.4 %); location-specific
  subject lines gave **18 % more opens**. **A/P** [Mailchimp benchmarks](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-marketing-benchmarks/), [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/average-email-open-rate-benchmark), [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)
- App push opt-in: **~61 % overall** (Android 67 %, iOS 56 %, falling); web push
  **5–10 %**. **A** [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/push-notifications/research/push-notifications-statistics/), [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/push-notification-opt-in-rate), [Airship benchmark PDF](https://grow.urbanairship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/WP_Push_Notification_Opt_In_Rates.pdf)
- Local intent exists: **71 % of adults want to meet more people in their neighbourhood**
  (Harris Poll for Nextdoor); **54 % are more likely to attend a locally trending event**.
  **A** [Nextdoor / Harris](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/blog/from-strangers-to-neighbors-unveiling-new-harris-poll-data-on-community-connectivity), [Eventbrite statistics](https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-statistics-ds00/)
- Frequency: DiscountDirect's research (already in this repo) found **four messages a
  month** is where most people unsubscribe — the cap applies here unchanged.

**Reading.** One weekly digest per consumer, built from saved items, location, age band
and the week's sessions, with click rate as the metric — not opens.

## 5. Generated pages: how listing sites earn search traffic

- Zillow: **~5.2 million indexed pages**, the vast majority generated per city,
  neighbourhood and property type; Tripadvisor's programmatic pages carry **~226 million
  organic visits a month**; the pattern is one page tree: country › region › city ›
  neighbourhood › category. **A** [GrackerAI on Zillow](https://gracker.ai/case-studies/zillow), [Practical Programmatic on Tripadvisor](https://practicalprogrammatic.com/examples/tripadvisor), [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/programmatic-seo)
- The sport platform already has the tree: 8 categories × 20 counties × 147 localities × 7
  age bands, with counts per facet from `/api/public/browse-facets` (`first-customer-classscout.md`).

**Reading.** The generated-pages automation is the platform's own SEO department; every
page must have real listings behind it (no empty category × place pages — a thin-content
risk the audit's facet counts prevent).

## 6. How listing platforms monetise the business side

- **Indeed**: sponsored jobs get **up to 5× more clicks** and are **65 % more likely to
  report a hire** than free posts; three free posts a month direct on Indeed. **P**
  [Indeed: free vs sponsored](https://www.indeed.com/hire/resources/howtohub/free-vs-sponsored-jobs-on-indeed), [Indeed pricing](https://www.indeed.com/hire/cs/pricing)
- **Mindbody marketplace**: pay **20 % (capped at $30) only on the first purchase of a
  new client** the app delivered; intro offers get premium placement. **P** [Mindbody app listing](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/List-your-Business-in-the-Mindbody-Marketplace?language=en_US)
- **Booksy**: subscription including messaging, free marketplace listing, a paid "Boost"
  for visibility. **P** [Booksy pricing](https://biz.booksy.com/pricing). **Treatwell**:
  10–20 % commission on completed bookings plus premium listings. **A** [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/create/how-to-build-website-like-treatwell/)
- **Yelp**: claiming is free and separate from advertising; the free tools are answering
  reviews and messages and seeing views and leads. **P** [Yelp: what is a claimed business](https://www.yelp-support.com/article/What-is-a-claimed-business?l=en_US), [Yelp for Business](https://business.yelp.com/products/business-page/)

**Reading.** The activation ladder every marketplace uses: free claim → free tools that
show value (reviews, messages, views) → paid visibility or pay-per-new-customer.
business.direct's platform view should show the ladder as states of a listing.

## 7. AI in the small business's marketing — adoption and the human in the loop

- Adoption: **54 % of US small businesses use AI marketing tools, 27 % more plan to**
  (US Chamber, Aug 2025); other 2026 surveys quote 73–87 %; primary uses content creation
  (48 %), customer-service automation (42 %), lead scoring (31 %). **A** [Capsule](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rogerdooley/2026/02/11/by-years-end-4-in-5-small-businesses-will-use-ai-marketing-tools/), [theStacc](https://thestacc.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/)
- Human-in-the-loop as the 2026 norm: agents **generate and stage, humans approve before
  publication**; approval stays mandatory for budget changes, sensitive copy and
  client-facing conclusions; Gartner: 40 % of enterprise apps will include task-specific
  agents by end-2026. **A** [Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai), [Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai-marketing/), [getclaw](https://getclaw.sh/blog/human-in-the-loop-ai-agents-approvals-2026)
- **EU AI Act, Article 50** (applies from 2 August 2026; machine-readable marking of
  generated text/images by 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market): AI
  chatbots must disclose they are AI; generated content must be marked. **P**
  [European Commission guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations), [Orrick summary](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/08/EU-AI-Act-Transparency-Obligations-for-AI-Generated-Content-Article-50)

**Reading.** "Optional AI support" (D6) is right on the evidence: the machine is rules
and integrations first; AI drafts where switched on, is labelled as AI where the law
requires, and never sends without the owner. The videos' rule and the regulation agree.

## 8. First market: New York (United States)

- **CAN-SPAM** requires no prior consent for commercial e-mail — B2B or B2C — but every
  message needs a working opt-out honoured within **10 business days**, a physical address,
  accurate headers and no deceptive subject lines. **P** [FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), [ANA FAQ](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/accountability-digital-marketing-CANSPAM-faq)
- **TCPA**: marketing **SMS needs prior express written consent** (signed, with clear
  disclosures; the FCC's 2025 one-to-one rule was vacated and the prior standard
  reinstated on 29 August 2025); opt-outs honoured within 10 business days from April
  2025; statutory damages **$500–1 500 per message**. **P/A** [FCC document](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-408396A1.pdf), [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/alerts/fcc-download/all/the-fcc-s-prior-express-written-consent-rule-is-changing-this-month-what-marketers-need-to-know), [BCLP on the opt-out rules](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/the-tcpas-new-opt-out-rules-take-effect-on-april-11-2025-what-does-this-mean-for-businesses.html)

**Reading.** Provider activation by e-mail (130 providers have an address on their card)
is lawful without consent; every family-facing SMS needs written consent captured on the
platform, and the frames show that as a state, not an assumption. E-mail and push carry
the digests; SMS only where a parent opted in for a specific provider.

## 8b. Reference market: Hungary

- **B2B e-mail to corporate addresses does not require prior consent** in Hungary under
  Act XLVIII of 2008 — unless the address contains personal data (a named person's
  address does); B2C requires express prior consent. **P** [DLA Piper: electronic marketing, Hungary](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=electronic-marketing&c=HU), [CMS guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/hungary)
- **57.4 % of Hungarian SMEs** have at least basic digital intensity (EU 72.9 %); AI use
  lags "in particular for SMEs, due to a lack of digital skills". **P** [Digital Decade 2025 country report, Hungary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/hungary-2025-digital-decade-country-report)

**Reading.** For the Hungarian reference instance the same activation is lawful to
`info@`-type addresses and not to a named owner without consent; the digital-skills gap
is the reason the machine must run with "nobody logs in" on any instance.

## 9. What the two reference videos add (owner-supplied, 2026-09-19)

Not evidence — a target shape, read from 42 extracted frames (`first-customer-classscout.md` §3):
departments as the unit of automation; a brand knowledge layer as plain files
(`voice-guide.md`, `positioning.md`, `offer.md`, `leads.md`); an orchestrator; an access
control plane; reviews and reputation answered first; a market radar that turns a
competitor's launch into a strategist's note; a daily recap; and the operating rule the
whole system obeys — *it drafts, it can't post, it stops and asks, the owner still owns,
everything else runs.*
