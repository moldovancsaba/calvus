1 · The customer — a classified media owner

A listing platform, a directory, a marketplace of local businesses: the owner has gathered the supply, built the site the visitors use, and is now the whole sales and marketing department for both sides of it. ClassScout, operator of Your Field NYC — a children's-activity directory in New York — is the example this presentation uses and the first customer.

Their supply

Thousands of listings, most not yet sold

Gathered by a pipeline from public sources; the businesses on them do not know they are listed, have not claimed their page, do not answer the enquiries the site sends them, and have not bought a featured placement. Every one is a prospect nobody has contacted.

Their demand

Visitors who save and leave

Visitors come, compare, save — and hear nothing: no "what's new near you", no alert when a saved listing changes, no content on the channels they actually use. The material for that content — sessions, offers, news, photos — already sits in the listings, unpublished.

Their week

One person, seven departments

Research the market. Write and publish content on every channel. Cut the video. Find advertisers, contact them, follow up three times. Answer every enquiry the same hour. Renew the ones about to leave. Read the numbers and decide where the next dollar goes. The tools on sale do one job each and leave the owner as the team.

2 · The complication — what the owner loses every week

Unsold inventory

A listing nobody has contacted earns nothing. Outbound to small businesses works — top-quartile reply rates are 5.5 %, top-decile 10.7 %, three touches in order triple the first touch — but only if somebody sends the second and third message, in the right order, within the law. One person sends the first and forgets the rest.

research II §5

Enquiries that die

The site sends a visitor to a business; the business answers late or never. A lead answered within five minutes is 21× more likely to qualify than after thirty; the visitor moves on — and blames the site.

MIT / InsideSales lead-response study; research I §2

Content that does not exist

Organic search sends 19 % fewer clicks to the same rankings; nearly half of Gen Z searches social first; AI answers cite the structured, reviewed source. A site that publishes nothing is not the source that gets cited or followed.

research II §1, §3

Churn nobody saw coming

A paying advertiser leaves for reasons visible weeks earlier — a stale page, an unanswered enquiry, a listing that stopped surfacing, a renewal that arrived without a result. Replacing one costs a full acquisition; keeping one costs a touch — if somebody is watching.

3 · The question

How does a classified media owner run marketing, B2B sales and retention every day for thousands of listings — without hiring a team, without losing control of what goes out under the site's name, and without breaking the law or the visitors' trust?

4 · The product — business.direct

Three jobs as departments, the owner's knowledge as plain files, a human gate on everything, one loop that ranks the next dollar, and the owner's economics as a screen.

Marketing — content to every medium

Generate and deliver

The product reads the listings — what is new, what is on offer, what starts this week, the photos and footage the advertisers upload — and drafts the week's content: posts for Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and X, a Sunday digest to visitors who opted in, alerts when a saved listing changes, neighbourhood × category pages for search and AI answers, clips cut from real footage with captions. You approve; it publishes at the slot, answers comments with a drafted reply, and reports what each piece brought.

B2B sales — contact and acquire

Every listing worked through a pipeline

Every listed business is scored and worked: identified → contacted → replied → applied → managing → upgraded. Three touches in propensity order from a warmed domain with the legal footer, opt-outs honoured, a call list for the phone-only, a drafted answer to every reply, a visitor's enquiry turned into the strongest sales touch, and the advertising products — featured placement, category placement, a discovery profile — offered when the product has delivered more than they cost.

Retention — reduce churn

Keep the ones who pay

Every managing advertiser is watched for the signals that precede a cancellation — a renewal coming, a page gone stale, an enquiry left unanswered, a listing that stopped surfacing — and a touch is drafted from the advertiser's own numbers before it happens. Renewal reminders arrive with results; kept and lost are logged and feed the churn rate on the Economics screen.

The advertiser's own team

What a managing advertiser gets

Once a business manages its page it gets its own one-person team inside your product: every enquiry answered within the hour from its own files, reminders that cut no-shows, campaigns to the visitors who saved it, clips from one recording, results on one screen. That is why they claim, why they stay, and why they upgrade.

The human gate

It drafts. It cannot send. It stops and asks.

Nothing reaches a visitor, an advertiser or a channel without an approval. Simple mode shows what needs you as ranked recommendations with one button; Advanced shows every screen and input. A department that runs four clean weeks earns auto-approval for routine messages — every one logged and stoppable.

The loop

Events → metrics → the next dollar → you → measure

Every touch, reply, post, save, enquiry, renewal and payment is an event. Nightly: the metrics. Weekly: four levers ranked by expected value per dollar — another touch, the call list, a week of content, retention touches. Monday: the recap says what moved and what to decide.

5 · How the owner uses it — twenty minutes a day, one screen on Monday

Every morning · Home

The product has drafted the day's work overnight. Home lists what needs a person, ranked, with the reason and one button: approve this week's posts and clips, send the invitation to the next batch, answer the replies, send the renewal reminders, review the at-risk advertisers, complete a missing policy field. One press runs every safe item; the judgement calls wait for you.

During the day · the inbox

Replies from prospects, enquiries from visitors, comments on posts — each arrives with a drafted answer from your files and the listing. Approve, edit in your own words, or skip. Editing removes the AI badge; the answer goes back on the same channel.

Monday · the recap

What moved: contacted, replied, applied, managing, upgraded, kept; posts published and what they brought; the market radar from your own catalogue; what needs a decision — and the next dollar, ranked.

Whenever · Economics

Change any input — a listing's price, the reply rate, churn — and every number follows: CAC, LTV, payback, the value of a visitor, cost per visitor from content, churn cost and retention value, the twelve-month plan. Every figure says whether it is measured, a benchmark or your assumption.

Onboarding · a week

Connect the site's API and the channels; fill the policy record (jurisdictions, consent per channel, the cap, the postal address, the privacy-policy clauses) — the product blocks any feature whose fields are missing and says why; write the voice, rules and offers as plain files; pick the templates. The second site is a record and a connector, not a rebuild.

Never · a login by a visitor

Visitors keep their account on your site; their preferences reach the product by signed link; every channel is off until they turn it on; Stop is one tap; a child is an age, never a name.

6 · The benefits — the owner's economics, in the product

Sold inventory

Every listing contacted, every reply answered, every ready advertiser offered the product at the moment it has earned it. The Economics screen shows the cost of winning an advertiser (CAC) against what it is worth (LTV) and the months to pay back — on your numbers, replacing the benchmarks as the first cohort arrives.

Visitors who come back

Content from what you already have, on the channels visitors use, with a digest and alerts they chose. The screen shows what a visitor costs to bring in and what an engaged visitor is worth to the advertisers and to you.

Advertisers who stay

Churn seen weeks ahead and answered with a touch, not a discount. The screen shows the LTV at risk, the share kept, and the retention lever ranked against acquisition — keeping costs a touch, replacing costs a CAC.

The next dollar, every week

Four levers ranked by expected value per dollar; the sequence cadence, the content slots and the moment the upgrade is offered follow the measured numbers. The product tells you the truth when a lever does not pay — the first instance's own model shows outbound alone not covering a $49 product at sample prices, and the three inputs that turn it.

Your time back

Seven departments' output for about twenty minutes of approvals a day; the operator's hours are measured on the screen and fall as departments earn auto-approval.

Lower exposure, more trust

One policy record per site and a gate in the code — consent per channel, the cap, the postal address, disclosure when a machine made something, no profiling of minors, no dark patterns, an audit trail on every send. The enforcement record in consumer marketing is a list of wrong defaults; the product makes the wrong default impossible to ship.

8 · Delivery

The build

Nine two-week sprints with an acceptance test at each: sales and conversations first, publishing when the channel review clears (submitted in sprint 0), visitors and the digest by sprint 6, campaigns, retention and payments by sprint 8, the second-site proof in sprint 9.

The service

Runs on the site's own stack family (Next.js on Vercel, MongoDB, the site's sign-in), e-mail, Instagram and Facebook, Stripe on the owner's account, a drafter behind an interface, one worker for video; every vendor behind an interface and verified.

What the first customer provides at onboarding

A postal address for the legal footer, one paragraph in their privacy policy (drafted by us), the policy record confirmed, counsel's wording on children's data; later their analytics events, a write key and a pilot advertiser.
