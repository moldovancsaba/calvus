253 providers listed. None manages its page. Families are told nothing. Here is how that changes.

Your Field has gathered the supply side of children's activities in New York and built the demand side's tools. What it does not have is the team that works both sides every day — answering, following up, publishing, selling. business.direct is that team, as a machine the platform runs: it drafts everything, sends nothing until a person approves, and measures every dollar.

3 · The question

How does a platform with no sales team and no content team turn 253 listings into managed pages, paying providers and families who come back every week — without breaking the law, a parent's trust, or the platform's own privacy policy?

4 · The answer — one machine, run by the platform, gated by a person

business.direct works both sides of the marketplace every day from the platform's own data, and shows the result in one place.

For the platform

Two flows at the front door

Families brought to the listing page by real content: the 83 sessions, 72 trial policies and 26 announcements become the week's posts and a Sunday "this week near you" digest. Providers brought to manage their page by a three-touch invitation in propensity order, from a warmed domain, with the law's footer and a cap — and every family's "ask about a trial" becomes the strongest sales touch of all.

For the provider

A one-person team it did not have

Every enquiry — a platform message, an e-mail, a missed call — answered from the coach's own files within the hour; the trial offer, the open Saturday spots and the club's news drafted as campaigns to the families who saved it; one session recording cut into the week's clips; results on one screen; the platform's reach products offered only once the machine has delivered more than they cost.

For the family

What arrives, and control over it

Posts and a digest built from what she saved; campaigns only from providers she saved, each saying why; a cap of four provider messages a month; every channel off by default; Stop in one tap. No child's name exists anywhere — a child is an age.

How it decides — the loop

Every touch, reply, post, save, ask and payment is an event → nightly metrics (cost per managing and per paying provider, lifetime value, the value of an avid family, cost per family from content) → the next dollar ranked across three levers (one more touch, the call list, a week of content) → a person approves → measure, then adjust the cadence, the content slots and the moment the upgrade is offered. The human gate never moves; it gets lighter — a week's clips are one decision, and a department that runs four clean weeks earns auto-approval for routine messages, every one logged and stoppable.

7 · Responsible by design — risk removed, value added

What you get

  • Lower exposure: the enforcement record in this category is a list of defaults and labels (Epic $520 M, YouTube $170 M, Disney $10 M); the machine's policy gate makes the wrong default impossible to ship.
  • A better audience: every message goes to a person who saved, asked or opted in — volunteered data converts better than inferred data and needs no profiling.
  • Trust as the channel: half of privacy-aware consumers have switched over data practices; 83 % of small businesses grow on referrals. The trusted platform is the one parents recommend.
  • An audit trail: every send carries its legal basis, its consent and its cap; a regulator's question is a query.

How it is done

  • A child is an age, never a name — matching Your Field's own policy (adults only, no children's data) and holding the line for any later client.
  • No profiling or targeting of minors, ever; no audience on a protected characteristic; no dark patterns; disclosure when a machine wrote or made something.
  • Real footage of children only with written parental consent; a coach's safeguarding shown as verified or not, never claimed; "not now" pauses everything and gets a human.
  • One policy record per client and a gate in the code: provider e-mail needs the postal address, the digest needs the privacy policy to describe it, SMS needs the consent text.

8 · Delivery — what it takes

Build

Nine two-week sprints, seventeen weeks, two developers with the owner as product lead; an acceptance test you can watch at every sprint review. Sales and conversations run first (sprint 2–3), social publishing when Meta's review clears (submitted in sprint 0), families and the digest by sprint 6, campaigns and upgrades by sprint 8.

Run cost

≈ $50–75 a month at pilot, ≈ $280–400 a month at 1 000 providers and 50 000 families — every vendor's own price, read on the day. Covered by two upgraded providers at pilot, eight at scale.

Stack

Your platform's own: Next.js on Vercel, MongoDB Atlas, your sign-in; e-mail, Instagram and Facebook, Stripe on your account; one small worker for video. Every service verified, every module specified with pseudo code.

What we need from you — after the decision

Nothing today. Then: the postal address for provider e-mail, one paragraph in the privacy policy (we draft it), the policy record confirmed, counsel's wording on children's data; later the opted-in share, the analytics events, a write key and one pilot provider.
