business.direct · prototype for Your Field NYC

The one-person marketing team, run by the platform

A listing platform is three parties: the platform, the listed business, the family. business.direct markets the businesses to families on social channels, and markets the platform to the businesses — drafts everything, sends nothing until a person approves, and shows every result in one place.

What is real, what is sample

Real: the catalogue

252 providers pulled from Your Field's public API on 2026-09-19 and never edited by hand: name, activity, neighbourhood, website, 180 phones, 130 e-mails, 83 next sessions, 72 trial policies, 26 announcements, claim state. The tiles, chips, pipeline totals, generated-page list and digest rows are computed from it.

Real: the evidence

The research page cites its sources for every figure: what an unanswered enquiry costs, reply-time studies, reminder effects on no-shows, how listing platforms monetise the business side, US CAN-SPAM and TCPA rules, EU AI Act Article 50.

Sample: the activity

Post copy, the invitation, the three replies, the provider's numbers, campaign audiences, product prices, Maya and her children, the SMS. Each is generated from real providers and labelled sample on the page. No provider or family was contacted.

Inert, on purpose

  • Sending, publishing and connecting an integration
  • Editing a sequence or a reply (a toast says so)
  • Persistence: reload resets the state

What we ask of you

Click through all three views on your phone; every correction becomes the next round of the build log.
Confirm the provider persona (Brooklyn Force Soccer) and the family persona (a Park Slope parent, children 5 and 9), or name others.
Integrations: we assumed v1 = the platform API, e-mail, Instagram + Facebook, push; TikTok, X, SMS, Google Business Profile and calendars later. Confirm or reorder.
Money: we assumed the base machine is bundled by the platform and the provider buys reach — your three "List your program" products at sample prices. Confirm the model and give the real prices.
Analytics: the family, sign-up-source, save and booking events, and the real product prices — each replaces one assumption in the economics model.
The platform's write endpoints (claim requests, notifications, saves for campaign audiences, card flags for upgrades): a key and the contract, or the prototype stays read-only.
Access to the platform's analytics (saves, plan usage, newsletter size) and, if a pilot provider agrees, its real numbers — today every non-catalogue figure is sample.
